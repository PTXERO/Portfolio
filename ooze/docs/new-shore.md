# New Shore — design

*The era after the Extinction. A game in its own right, and a long one.*

Status: **planning** (nothing built yet). Decisions made so far are written as decided; open
questions are collected at the end. Keep this up to date as we decide things.

---

## 1. In one paragraph

Four survivors come ashore on a big, fogged island that is already lived on. They found a
village around a hearth and grow it toward a hundred people, while the island's other villages
either join them, flee, or fight to the death — which of those depends on diet. At about a
hundred, the game turns: the village becomes a people, and the long haul begins — an idle game
about developing a culture across ages, settling the whole island, until it stands as a
Roman-era republic of many kinds.

## 2. Pillars

1. **Idle at heart.** Everything progresses with no one watching. Being there makes it faster
   and gives you the defining choices; it never gates progress. (See §8.)
2. **Belonging.** The Creature was about roaming, the Extinction about surviving; New Shore is
   about claiming a land and deciding what you'll be with the people already on it.
3. **Diet is destiny.** The line's diet (and cannibalism) decides who can join, who must be
   fought, how you eat, how you fight, and which road your culture walks.
4. **Always moving, always something to do.** The pace never sags: something visible changes
   every few minutes, and there's always a choice waiting at some timescale.
5. **It remembers.** What happened before (the spear, the crossing, the four, the Chronicle)
   becomes this people's history and myth.

## 3. Carried over from earlier eras

- **The four** who crossed, with the player's look (parts, colours) and diet.
- **Diet**, including a permanent cannibal line.
- **The Chronicle**: it carries on, and becomes the source of the culture's Stories.
- **Ideas the spear taught** in the Extinction: head starts (fire, rope, racks…).
- **The new island**: 8× the size of the spear's, under fog of war; a column of dark smoke
  from the nearest village on landing.
- **Hut and fire**: the strange stars on the crossing foreshadowed them.

## 4. Phase 1 — Settling (4 → about 100 people)

The more active half. Found the village, scout the island, deal with every other village.

### 4.1 The village

- It **lays itself out around the hearth**: you choose what to build (idle-style lists), and it
  appears in place. No hand placement.
- Growth: births need food to spare and room in huts. The village gathers by itself; you
  choose builds and priorities.
- **The four die of old age**, but only after births have begun (the line always goes on).
  Each death is a moment: a grave, a Chronicle entry.
- **A grace period**: enemy scouts don't find you until you're a little established (≈12
  people and a first fire).

### 4.2 The island's villages

6–7 villages of 8–40 people at the start, hidden in the fog, each with a diet and its own kind
(different parts, colours). The mix is chosen against the player's diet so there are always
some to win over (except for cannibals) and at least one predator.

**The food chain**: carnivore → omnivore → herbivore (each preys on the next).

| They are… | What happens |
|---|---|
| Same diet | **Diplomacy**: win them over until they **join** (people, land, a new kind). |
| Your prey | **You raid**: you choose when to attack. |
| Your predator | **They raid you**: you defend. Prey *can* wipe out a predator, but only by numbers — hard. |
| (You're cannibals) | **Everyone is an enemy**; none ever joins. Every battle won turns the dead into meat. Weaker villages may flee your borders instead. |

**Every hostile village ends one of three ways: it joins, it flees the island, or it fights to
the death.**

### 4.3 Winning a village over

A Trust meter per village. Raised by gifts, feasts, helping with their troubles (a sick chief,
a bad harvest), marriages between villages, showing them your ideas; knocked back by bad
events. Full Trust: they move in.

### 4.4 Raiding and defending

- **Raids** play out over a short siege bar; win and you take their land and stores, lose and
  you lose people.
- **Defense**: raids are announced with a countdown (like the Extinction's crises) — time to
  raise walls, arm defenders, pull people in.
- Prey win against predators by holding enough raids off, then attacking with numbers.

### 4.5 New kinds

Kinds who join stay visibly different in the village (other legs, jaws, colours), and each
brings a strength (tusked kinds make warriors, keen-eyed kinds scout further, seafaring kinds
build boats…). Who joins in Phase 1 tilts the culture in Phase 2.

### 4.6 The chief

You play the tribe's leader (one of the four, then their heirs), walking the map like the
Creature. Being there counts: gifts brought in person count double; raids you lead hit
harder. Choices come as decisions between chief and tribe.

## 5. The Far Voice (the spear, from far away)

The spear's will becomes a faith — "the Far Voice":

- **Whispers**, rarely: a choice every few days at most (they time out to the elders, as now).
- **Two or three powers**, weaker than in the Extinction, charged by devotion from the
  culture's Rites and Stories lanes.
- How loud it is depends on the culture's **stance on the spear** (a fork in Rites, §6.4):
  awe (more whispers, stronger powers), fear (obedience, but dread), or forgetting (no powers
  at all, but Words and Rule develop much faster).

*To design: the powers themselves, and the whisper set.*

## 6. Phase 2 — Culture (100 → the Republic)

The long haul, idle at heart. At about a hundred people a short moment marks the turn (a great
fire, everyone gathered), and Culture becomes the thing you build. Any villages still standing
remain: predators still raid now and then (met by your defenses), same-diet villages can still
drift in, and every one of them must still end in joining, fleeing or a fight to the death.

### 6.1 The Long Road

A **side-scrolling timeline that develops by itself**, from the Hearth Age on the left to the
Republic on the right. Eight horizontal **lanes**, one per part of life; a glowing **frontier**
moving right as the culture develops; the future fogged ahead of it; big bands for the Ages;
a row of **Great Works** along the bottom. Where a lane forks, you choose; **the road not taken
stays on the road, greyed out**, so you can see what you gave up.

### 6.2 Lanes

| Lane | Covers |
|---|---|
| Food | How you eat (the most diet-specific lane) |
| Crafts | Tools, materials, metal, building |
| War | Fighting, defending, raiding, armies |
| Rule | How decisions are made |
| Kin | Living with the other kinds who joined |
| Rites | Faith, festivals, the Far Voice |
| Stories | Memory, myth, history |
| Words | Counting, marks, writing, records |

**Great Works**: the row of monuments that close each Age.

### 6.3 How ideas develop: practice

- Each lane gathers **practice** from what people actually do (jobs, buildings, settlements).
  Potters practise Crafts, elders Stories, the longhouse Rule.
- When a lane's practice fills, its next idea completes **on its own**.
- You steer by **focusing** a lane, and by building and settling what feeds it.
- **Sparks**: events jump-start ideas (a flood → a rain rite; a war won → a war song; a famine
  → a granary; a new kind joining → a shared craft).
- **The pace never sags**: practice grows with people and settlements, so ideas keep landing at
  a steady rhythm; later Ages have more, smaller steps rather than longer waits (never more than
  ≈20–30 minutes without something new).

### 6.4 Forks (choices that make your people yours)

About a dozen, permanent. First list:

- **The dead**: bury · burn · *(cannibals)* eat.
- **The spear**: awe · fear · forget (§5).
- **Strangers**: welcome (more joiners, more variety) · guard (stronger defense, unity).
- **Rule**: council of elders · one chief · assembly of all.
- **Wealth**: held in common · owned and traded.
- **War**: war band (raids) · militia (defense) · *(later)* standing army (toward legions).

### 6.5 The Ages

| Age | Moves on when… |
|---|---|
| 1. The Hearth | the village stands (camp → village) |
| 2. Clay & Field | pottery, fields or herds, a longhouse |
| 3. Bronze | copper and tin sites settled; the first hall and shrine |
| 4. Iron | the bog-iron site; roads between settlements; a harbour |
| 5. The Republic | a city, every settlement joined by road, one people of many kinds |

To move to the next Age: key ideas done in at least four lanes, the Age's Great Work built,
and its resources in hand. **The era ends at the Republic.**

First pass of ideas per Age (to be expanded per diet, §7):

| Age | Stories | Rites | Crafts | Words | Rule | Kin | War | Great Works |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hearth | The Crossing told; the spear far off | Burial / burning; first feast | Baskets; dried meat; stone blades | Names; finger-counting | The four's word; elders' circle | Mixed hearths; shared songs | Watchers; clubs | Great Hearth |
| Clay & Field | Ancestors; the four as legends | Seasons kept; harvest festival | Pottery; weaving | Tallies; signs on pots | Council; laws spoken | Marriage between kinds | Palisade; spears | Longhouse; standing stones |
| Bronze | Heroes of the island wars | Priests of the Far Voice | Copper → bronze; the wheel | Picture marks; trade tallies | Chiefs and households; tribute | Clans by kind; guild lines | Bronze arms; shields | Chief's hall; first shrine |
| Iron | Epics; the island's story | Temples; the calendar | Iron; the plough; ships | Written law; scribes | One council over many villages; roads | Citizens and strangers; a common tongue | Iron arms; forts | Roads; harbour |
| Republic | Myth made history | State festivals; oaths | Concrete; arches; coin | Schools; records; maps | Assembly / senate; magistrates | One people of many kinds | Drilled army (legions / chariots / walled towns / terror) | Forum, Circus, or League city; aqueduct |

### 6.6 Settling the island

Later Ages need what's out on the island, so the map fills from one village to a web of
settlements. **Points of interest**: copper and tin seams, an iron bog, a quarry, salt flats, a
sacred grove, river mouths (harbours), hunting grounds, fertile valleys… You found daughter
settlements at them and join them with roads. Many POIs, many villages: the whole island ends
up settled.

## 7. Diet: four roads

Each diet has its own Food and War lanes, its own versions of some ideas elsewhere, and a
signature mechanic.

### Hunters (carnivores) — aggression pays
- **Food**: hunting only, never farming: tracking → pack hunts → drives → game runs (managing
  the island's wild herds) → great hunts.
- **Glory** (signature): earned only by aggression (raids won, great hunts, champions); feeds
  War and Stories and sets off festivals. **Racing**: hunt-festival races grow into
  beast-riding, then chariot races, and in the end the **Circus**.
- **War**: ambush → war bands → swift raiders → chariots. Raids recharge faster; every win
  feeds Glory.
- **Rule**: the strongest leads; challenges settled by contest.
- **Kin**: winning villages over is slow, but captives can become kin in time.
- **Weakness**: no farming; food rises and falls with the hunt unless the game runs are tended.
- **Republic**: the Circus.

### Omnivores — the balance
- **Food**: farming and ranching only (no hunting, no foraging): fields → herds → rotation →
  dairy and wool → irrigation → estates.
- **Accord and Arms** (signature): both diplomacy and force, and the tree rewards keeping them
  in balance (a lean too far either way loses a bonus that matching them gives).
- Markets and roads early; law → magistrates; militia → drilled infantry → legions.
- **Republic**: the Forum (senate, roads, legions) — the most Roman.

### Grazers (herbivores) — the welcoming people
- **Food**: foraging and farming only (no herds, no hunting): gathering → gardens → terraces →
  orchards → irrigation → great gardens.
- **Welcome** (signature): joining is **passive** — same-diet villages near you drift toward you
  over time, faster with every Kin and Rites idea.
- **War**: almost all defense (hiding places → palisades → watchers → walls → fortified towns).
  Grazers win by outlasting, not chasing; "by numbers" against predators fits them best.
- **Republic**: the League — free towns, temples, gardens, an aqueduct city.

### Cannibals
- Walk the base road of the diet they came from, but **Fear** replaces Kin, and diplomacy
  is gone.
- **Dread** (signature): weaker villages flee cannibal borders (leaving land); every battle won
  fills the stores.
- Rites and Stories turn dark (feast-rites, trophy halls, the Eaten Gods).
- **Republic**: a feared empire ruled by terror — powerful, and alone.

## 8. Idle and active

**Rule: an idle player reaches the Republic; an active player gets there faster and makes it
more their own.** Nothing waits forever on a person.

| System | Active (you're there) | Idle (you're not) |
|---|---|---|
| Gathering, births, building | Choose builds and priorities | The village gathers and builds by itself; the **Steward** upgrades let elders build by priorities you set |
| Scouting | Walk the chief into the fog | **Scout** upgrades: scouts explore on their own and mark what they find |
| Winning villages over | Visit, choose gifts and gestures (in person ×2) | **Envoy** upgrades: trade runs raise Trust steadily; grazers' Welcome works by itself |
| Raids | Launch and lead them (bonus when you lead) | **War chief** upgrades: raid by a policy you set ("weaker villages only", "whenever ready") |
| Defense | Rally defenders when a raid comes (tap bonus) | Always runs, offline too: walls and defenders hold by themselves |
| Culture (the Long Road) | Focus lanes, use sparks | Practice builds and ideas complete on their own, offline too |
| Forks | Choose | A fork never blocks the road: other lanes go on, and after a while the elders choose by the culture's leaning (with a Chronicle note) — like the Extinction's whispers |
| Festivals | Time them for the best boost | Rites ideas (the calendar) hold them by themselves, for a smaller boost |
| Settlements | Found them where you like | Steward upgrades found them at the best open POI |
| Whispers | Answer | Time out to the elders |

**Safe while away**: offline, the village gathers, grows and defends; it only raids or moves
on a village if you've bought the upgrade that lets it. You never come back to a disaster you
had no say in — unless you chose to let it run.

**Active is a multiplier, not a gate**: being there is worth roughly ×1.5–2 on pace (timed
festivals, focused lanes, sparks used at once, gifts in person), plus the choices that shape
your people.

## 9. Pacing (long haul)

- Phase 1: hours (to be tuned).
- Phase 2: early Ages ≈ 1–2 days of real time each, the Republic Age ≈ a week.
- Something visible changes every few minutes, always: an idea lands (and shows on the map:
  fields, pots, the first road, banners, a forge glow, festival fires…), a ticker of culture
  news, the Long Road lighting up.
- Always something to do, at every timescale:

| Scale | Waiting for you |
|---|---|
| Minutes | a festival to time, a raid or hunt, a lane to focus, a spark |
| Hours | a fork, a new settlement, a village to win or fight |
| Days | an Age turning and its Great Work, a whisper, the last villages |
